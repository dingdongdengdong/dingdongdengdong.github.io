01 / Research direction

The project is not just an arm—it is a controlled path from human demonstration to physical action.

A learned policy is only useful when the robot model, observations, action definition, controller ownership, and safety state all describe the same physical system.

Off-the-shelf learning examples assume a supported robot, stable cameras, a known gripper, and a ready dataset interface. This project starts from the opposite condition: a custom embodiment whose mechanics and software contract must be established together.

The first target is intentionally narrow—see a cube, approach it, enclose it, lift it by at least 5 cm, and hold it for at least 0.5 seconds. A narrow task makes synchronization errors, unreachable poses, weak grasps, and unsafe command transitions measurable instead of hiding them behind a general “robot AI” claim.

01

Embodiment

Keep URDF joints, limits, tool frames, camera mounts, and policy dimensions under one versioned contract.

02

Demonstration

Record what the robot actually sensed and measured—not simulation-only state or a commanded pose disguised as feedback.

03

Learning

Predict a short future while repeatedly returning to fresh images and measured joint state.

04

Deployment

Place teleoperation and policy commands behind one fail-closed authority boundary before motors.

02 / Hardware evolution

V1 proves the path. V2 expands the robot contract without pretending the new rollout is complete.

V1 · validated foundation3-axis + AmazingHand

The first working stack maps Quest motion through IK and safety to J1/J2/J3, records front and wrist RGB, and uses an AmazingHand grasp command as the fourth action value.

  • Three measured DM motor joint angles
  • AmazingHand command without fabricated hand-state feedback
  • Quest clutch, trigger, reset, and HOLD behavior
  • 15 Hz LeRobot episode recorder
V2 · current build5-axis + two-finger gripper

V2 keeps the learning direction while expanding the embodiment to five Damiao DM-J4340P joints and a Feetech STS3215 parallel gripper.

  • arm_v2_yaw180_ik.urdf for Placo IK
  • Visual URDF retains the gripper mechanism
  • Yaw-180 neutral aligns cable-safe J1 with zero
  • Physical signs, gains, offsets, and gripper range still require calibration

03 / Teleoperation & safety

Quest and the learned policy never compete for the same physical connection.

INPUT

WebXR mapping

Right-controller pose supplies the end-effector target. Grip acts as a clutch, trigger controls grasp, and explicit home/HOLD controls avoid unintended motion when tracking begins.

CONTROL

IK + bounded motion

Targets pass through workspace, joint-limit, velocity, divergence, and watchdog checks. The model never writes directly to CAN motors.

FAIL SAFE

HOLD before fallback

Loss of state, images, chunks, heartbeat, timing, or connectivity freezes new tracking. A TRIP remains a manual-clear condition.

04 / Demonstration data

The dataset is a synchronized record of what the robot saw, measured, and was asked to do.

Observation / visionFront RGB + wrist RGB

The front view captures global workspace context; the wrist view preserves the near-field relationship between gripper and object.

Observation / stateMeasured joint feedback

CAN feedback represents the robot state. A command is never copied into observation as if the mechanism had already reached it.

ActionJoint targets + grasp

V1 records J1/J2/J3 plus grasp. V2 expands this interface to five arm axes and the parallel-gripper command.

Episode qualityMotion, view, reset, outcome

Stationary, occluded, collided, or poorly reset episodes are rejected or preserved separately as failures—not relabeled as good demonstrations.

05 / Echo Flow Lite

A compact flow-matching policy built for this robot—not a renamed general-purpose VLA.

The model borrows the useful flow-matching and sim-to-real direction of the LeHome Challenge work, while deliberately remaining smaller and auditable.

During training, the correct action chunk is mixed with Gaussian noise at a sampled flow time. The transformer learns the velocity connecting data to noise. At inference, ten Euler steps reverse that path from noise toward a usable action sequence.

Optional success and progress heads support later hard-example mining and simulation AWR. Missing labels are masked rather than invented. Echo Flow Lite contains no language model and is not the complete LeHome, π0.5, or OpenPI system.

06 / Simulation-to-real gates

Rendering, contact, and learned behavior are separate claims—and each needs its own test.

01

Structural proof

URDF hierarchy, joints, limits, mass, collisions, tool frames, and camera mounts load as intended.

Evidence: model inspection + configuration validation
02

Mechanical proof

A scripted controller approaches, encloses, lifts, and holds the object with stable contact.

Promotion target: at least 18 successful trials out of 20
03

Data proof

Images, feedback, actions, timestamps, resets, and outcomes remain synchronized and replayable.

Evidence: 20-episode collection smoke + inspection
04

Behavior proof

A frozen policy succeeds on fixed placements and seeds without safety-bucket regression.

Not yet claimed: no trained V2 checkpoint or rollout rate
